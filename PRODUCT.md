# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Hiring managers and engineering leaders at London companies evaluating Jason for senior/principal frontend roles. They arrive with a CV or LinkedIn profile already in hand and limited time; the site is where they decide whether the craft claims are real. Their context is evaluative and skeptical — they've seen a hundred dev portfolios and are looking for the signal that this one is different.

## Product Purpose

Jason Wright's personal portfolio: a single-page site (Work + Projects) that demonstrates, rather than asserts, design-grade frontend craft. Success is concrete — a visitor reaches out or advances Jason in a hiring process for a senior/principal frontend position, timed around the Tokyo → London move in 2026.

## Positioning

The rare engineer whose UI polish and motion work is design-grade — and the site itself is the proof. Every screen reinforces motion and interface craft as the differentiator.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: connect or message on LinkedIn (https://www.linkedin.com/in/jaswright/).
- Secondary CTA: explore a flagship project (Observe / Cedar, ds-bench) — the work keeps unconvinced visitors engaged.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Engineer who ships design-grade UI."
- Belief ladder: (1) this person has genuine design-grade UI/motion craft → (2) they operate at senior/principal engineering depth (React, systems, modern stack) → (3) they're credible and low-risk (real career history, commercial leadership) → (4) they're available and worth contacting now (London 2026).
- Proof on hand: live project demos (Observe at cedar-ui-observe.vercel.app, and the other deployed projects in `src/content/projects/`), career history (`src/data/career.ts`), and public open-source repos on GitHub (github.com/jwrighty).

## Brand Personality

Expressive, kinetic, playful. The site performs its own argument: motion and interaction details carry the personality, while the voice stays confident and human. Reference lane: minimal text-first portfolios in the spirit of paco.me and rauno.me — sparse, typographic, with interaction craft over decoration. The expressiveness lives in how things move and respond, not in visual noise.

## Anti-references

- The generic dev-portfolio template: hero banner, skills grid, identical project-card wall, contact form.
- Awwwards showcase excess: scroll-hijacking, WebGL noise, motion for its own sake that buries the content.
- Dark-mode SaaS landing grammar: gradient glows, glassmorphism cards, startup-landing patterns applied to a person.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, don't claim.** Craft is demonstrated in the interface itself — an interaction that delights is worth more than a paragraph saying "I care about detail."
2. **Kinetic restraint.** Motion is the signature, but it serves reading and comprehension; expressiveness lives in response and detail, never in spectacle that slows the evaluative visitor down.
3. **Text-first, interaction-rich.** Sparse typographic surfaces where the delight is in how elements respond — the paco.me/rauno.me lane, not the showcase lane.
4. **Ladder every screen.** Each surface should advance the belief ladder: craft → depth → credibility → availability. If an element advances none of them, cut it.
5. **The demo is the proof.** Link out to live, clickable work early and often; deployed projects are the evidence base.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: ≥4.5:1 body contrast, full keyboard navigation, and a genuine `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for every animation — non-negotiable for a motion-led portfolio, where accessible motion is itself a proof of craft.
